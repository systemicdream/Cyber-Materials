What is cryptography? (literal Greek meaning)	Secret writing — the art and science of concealing meanings	crypto w1
The unscrambled, readable input to encryption is called __________; the scrambled output is called __________.	plaintext; ciphertext	crypto w1
What is a cryptosystem composed of?	A set of plaintexts, a set of keys, a set of ciphertexts, plus an enciphering function	crypto w1
What is cryptanalysis?	The science of breaking codes — analyzing encrypted data and finding/correcting weaknesses in cryptographic algorithms	crypto w1
Who is the most famous historic figure in cryptanalysis, known for cracking codes in WWII?	Alan Turing	crypto w1
An algorithm should not rely on __________ for its security; only the __________ needs to be kept secret.	its secrecy; the key (Kerckhoffs-style principle)	crypto w1
Why should encryption algorithms be made public for scrutiny?	More eyes on the algorithm verifies it works and builds trust; secrecy of the algorithm should not be required for security	crypto w1
In a ciphertext-only attack, what do you have and what is the goal?	You have only the ciphertext; goal is to find the plaintext (the key may also be found along the way)	crypto w1
In a known-plaintext attack, what do you have and what is the goal?	You have both the ciphertext and the plaintext; goal is to find the key	crypto w1
In a chosen-plaintext attack, what do you do and what is the goal?	You choose specific plaintexts to be encrypted and receive the resulting ciphertexts; goal is to find the key by spotting patterns	crypto w1
Why is finding the key in a known-plaintext attack valuable?	The same key likely encrypts future messages, so you can decrypt all subsequent traffic	crypto w1
How can attacks be accomplished (two general methods)?	Math skills or statistical methods (statistical assumptions about the plaintext language are called the model)	crypto w1
Symmetric encryption is also called __________ or __________ encryption.	single-key; classical (one key encrypts and decrypts)	crypto w1
In symmetric encryption, how many keys are used?	One — the same key encrypts and decrypts	crypto w1
The two categories of classical (symmetric) ciphers are __________ and __________.	transposition; substitution	crypto w1
What does a transposition cipher do?	Rearranges the characters of the plaintext without changing them	crypto w1
How is the rail fence cipher created?	Write the plaintext down two balanced columns (first half in column 1, second half in column 2), then read across rows one at a time	crypto w1
How can you detect that a transposition cipher was used?	The ciphertext contains normal English letter frequencies (many E, T, A, O, I; few J, Q, X, Z) — letters were rearranged, not replaced	crypto w1
What are the most common letters in English (in order)?	E (most common), then T, A, O, I	crypto w1
What are the least common letters in English?	J, Q, X, Z	crypto w1
Besides single-letter frequency, what other frequency table helps attack transposition ciphers?	Letter-pair (digram) frequencies — common pairs like TH, ER, ON, AN	crypto w1
What does a substitution cipher do?	Replaces characters with different characters (letters are changed, not reordered)	crypto w1
How can you tell a substitution cipher from a transposition cipher?	Substitution ciphertext has abnormal letter frequencies (letters changed); transposition has normal frequencies (letters just jumbled)	crypto w1
In the Caesar cipher, the key is the __________.	offset (shift amount) — typically 3	crypto w1
To decrypt a Caesar cipher with shift 3, you __________ each letter by 3.	subtract (shift back)	crypto w1
Why was the Caesar cipher effective in its time?	Most people couldn't read, so only a small shift was needed to conceal messages	crypto w1
How does frequency analysis break a Caesar cipher?	Compute each letter's frequency in the ciphertext, multiply by expected English frequencies, sum for each shift, and pick the shift with the highest total	crypto w1
What is the maximum shift in a Caesar cipher?	25 (shift of 26 rotates back to the original letters)	crypto w1
The Vigenère cipher is an improved __________ cipher that uses a __________ to vary the shift.	Caesar (substitution); keyword	crypto w1
How does the Vigenère cipher determine each letter's shift?	Each letter of the repeating keyword gives a shift amount (A=0, B=1, ..., Z=25) — look up the intersection of the keyword letter's row and the plaintext letter's column in the Vigenère table	crypto w1
When decrypting with Vigenère, you use the keyword letter to pick a __________ and trace down to the ciphertext letter's __________.	column; row (the row-column intersection gives the plaintext letter)	crypto w1
What is the key weakness of the Vigenère cipher?	Repeating patterns: when the key's characters align the same way over repeated ciphertext characters, the repeated ciphertext reveals the key length (a multiple of the spacing)	crypto w1
Does a longer or shorter Vigenère key make the cipher stronger?	Longer — it produces fewer repetitions, making the pattern harder to detect	crypto w1
How can a substitution cipher be made stronger?	Combine substitution and transposition (encrypt with one, then encrypt the result with the other) — the basis of modern layered encryption	crypto w1
The Nazi German cipher machine used in WWII was called the __________.	Enigma	crypto w1
What was the critical flaw of the Enigma machine?	A letter can never be encrypted to itself (pressing K never produces K)	crypto w1
What German habit helped the Allies crack Enigma?	Reusing phrases in messages (e.g., weather reports always started the same way), providing known plaintext	crypto w1
Asymmetric cryptography is also known as __________ cryptography.	public key	crypto w1
Who introduced public key cryptography and when?	Whitfield Diffie and Martin Hellman in 1976	crypto w1
In asymmetric cryptography, what do you encrypt with and what do you decrypt with?	Encrypt with the recipient's public key; decrypt with the corresponding private key	crypto w1
If a message is encrypted with a private key, how is it decrypted?	Only with the corresponding public key (this is the basis of digital signing)	crypto w1
What is the big problem with symmetric key encryption that asymmetric solves?	Key exchange — securely sharing the single key (interception risk), plus poor scalability	crypto w1
What is out-of-band messaging?	Exchanging information (like a key) using a transmission method other than the usual/expected channel	crypto w1
What is the formula for the number of keys needed for n people using symmetric encryption?	n × (n − 1) / 2 (e.g., 3 people → 3 keys; 1000 people → 499,500 keys)	crypto w1
What is the main advantage of asymmetric cryptography?	No key distribution problem and better scalability — each person has just one public/private pair instead of a key per communication pair	crypto w1
What is the main disadvantage of asymmetric cryptography?	It is slow — up to 1000 times slower than symmetric algorithms due to complicated math	crypto w1
How does hybrid encryption combine symmetric and asymmetric cryptography?	Asymmetric crypto exchanges a symmetric session key (encrypted with the receiver's public key); the fast symmetric key then encrypts the actual message traffic	crypto w1
In HTTPS/TLS, how is asymmetric cryptography used?	Only once, at the start — to establish the connection and pass the symmetric key; the rest of the session uses the symmetric key	crypto w1
Which classified British military research preceded public public-key cryptography, and who is credited?	James Ellis conceived it (couldn't implement it); Clifford Cocks created an RSA-like algorithm from Ellis's idea	crypto w1
RSA was developed at MIT by __________, __________, and __________.	Ron Rivest, Adi Shamir, and Leonard Adleman (Rivest–Shamir–Adleman)	crypto w1
What one-way function is RSA based on?	Multiplying two large primes is fast; factoring the product back into its primes takes enormously longer (millions/billions of times)	crypto w1
What is a one-way (forward vs. reverse) function?	A function whose forward computation is fast but whose inverse takes enormously longer (e.g., multiply two primes vs. factor the product)	crypto w1
Why do larger prime numbers make RSA stronger?	The larger the key/numbers, the greater the gap between forward and inverse computation time — factoring takes far longer	crypto w1
Is public-key encryption impossible to break?	No — it's just computationally infeasible; with enough processing power it could be broken, so faster future machines may threaten it	crypto w1
Why does signing a message with your private key prove your identity?	Only your private key can produce something decryptable by your public key — so successful decryption with your public key proves you encrypted it	crypto w1
How do you both sign a message and keep it confidential (both parties have key pairs)?	Encrypt with your private key (sign), then encrypt the result with the recipient's public key; the recipient decrypts with their private key, then with your public key to verify	crypto w1
