What was phone phreaking?	Hacking the phone system — the dominant form of hacking around 1969, before most people had home computers.	w4 history
What famous cereal-box toy was used to hack the phone system, and how?	The Captain Crunch whistle — blown into a phone receiver, it produced a 2600 Hz tone that activated and controlled phone trunk lines.	w4 history
What was the original connotation of the term "hacker"?	Positive — a clever experimenter who made computers do unthinkable things (games, music, graphics); the negative meaning came later.	w4 history
Who is considered the greatest all-around hacker for designing both hardware and software (and co-founding Apple)?	Steve Wozniak	w4 history
What low-tech technique was Kevin Mitnick famous for?	Social engineering — talking his way into places and persuading people on the phone to divulge passwords and other secrets.	w4 history
What is the title of Kevin Mitnick's book about social engineering?	The Art of Deception	w4 history
According to NIST, what is information security?	The protection afforded to an automated information system to preserve its confidentiality, integrity, and availability.	w4
What three properties make up the CIA triad?	Confidentiality, Integrity, Availability	w4
Which member of the CIA triad protects against eavesdropping and data theft?	Confidentiality	w4
Which member of the CIA triad protects against data corruption and tampering?	Integrity	w4
Which member of the CIA triad protects against service denial and data loss (reliability)?	Availability	w4
Why must confidentiality, integrity, and availability be balanced rather than maximized individually?	Locking computers in a dark, unplugged room boosts confidentiality and integrity but destroys availability — a secure system needs the right amount of all three.	w4
What does PII stand for, and what are examples?	Personally Identifiable Information — credit card numbers, bank account numbers, social security numbers.	w4
What are examples of intellectual property that confidentiality must protect?	Source code, customer databases, technical specifications, patents, copyrights, secret formulas.	w4
What is a masquerader?	Someone pretending to be a real user/customer — e.g., using a stolen identity or stolen username and password.	w4
What is a Trojan horse?	Legitimate-seeming software (e.g., a PDF) that secretly carries malicious code such as a keylogger.	w4
What integrity incident hit Apple when its iOS compiler was altered?	Attackers modified the compiler so malware was injected into iOS applications built with it.	w4
What principle says users should be granted only the minimum access needed to do their job?	Least privilege / need-to-know	w4
What is separation of duties?	No single employee controls a transaction from beginning to end — e.g., the person altering data isn't the one checking its accuracy.	w4
What is rotation of duties?	Periodically changing employees' job assignments so one person isn't permanently in control of the same function.	w4
What is a denial-of-service (DoS) attack?	An attack in which the intruder ties up a system so legitimate users cannot use it.	w4
What is a botnet?	A network of tens or hundreds of thousands of compromised "zombie" computers controlled by an attacker to flood a target (as in DDoS).	w4
What is a zombie computer?	A computer infected with malware that lets a malicious user remotely control it (often as part of a botnet).	w4
Why is buying security often compared to buying insurance?	Like insurance, you pay for protection against an attack that may never happen — making it hard to know how much to spend.	w4
Why is security usually an afterthought?	It isn't designed in from the beginning because it slows product release and is seen as an impediment to shipping quickly.	w4
What is meant by "people are the problem" in security?	The best firewalls and cryptography fail if a user gives away a password or opens a path around protections.	w4
Why is inserting a found USB drive into a computer so dangerous?	The drive can carry malware that immediately infects the machine (the same trick used in attacks like Stuxnet).	w4 domains
Why do attackers often target the CEO's workstation?	The CEO typically has the highest privileges but relatively low technical skill — the "keys to the kingdom."	w4 domains
What is Patch Tuesday and how do attackers exploit it?	The day Microsoft releases patches — attackers reverse-engineer the patches to learn the vulnerabilities and attack systems that haven't yet been patched.	w4 domains
How can someone get onto a wired LAN without credentials?	Physically tapping into a live open network port — wired LAN access typically grants more access than wireless.	w4 domains
What can you see on an unencrypted wireless network (e.g., a coffee shop)?	All other users' traffic in cleartext — unless they're using HTTPS, which encrypts it.	w4 domains
What tool is used for unauthorized probing and port scanning?	nmap — it reveals what servers are open and what services they run.	w4 domains
Why are Telnet and FTP considered insecure?	They transmit data (including login credentials) unencrypted in cleartext, enabling eavesdropping.	w4 domains
How can a security team neutralize phishing links in emails users have already clicked?	Disable the link on the internal network so it no longer resolves to the malicious site.	w4 domains
What is a brute-force attack on remote access, and one mitigation?	Trying every combination of user ID and password — mitigated by limiting login retries and locking out the IP after repeated failures.	w4 domains
What US law protects student data like grades and GPAs?	FERPA — only people whose job requires it (advisor, registrar) should access a student's GPA.	w4 domains
Why are servers requiring high availability a security threat?	They're hard to take offline to patch, so they may stay vulnerable.	w4 domains
Which security technology is most used, and which is least used?	Most used: antivirus software. Least used: biometrics.	w4
How do smart cards strengthen authentication?	They add a second factor (something you have) to the password, enabling two-factor authentication.	w4
What is Stuxnet, and why is it significant?	A cyber-physical attack that crossed from digital to physical, causing actual damage to Iran's nuclear facility.	w4
In the defender-attacker asymmetry, who has the harder job and why?	The defender — they must find and eliminate ALL exploitable vulnerabilities, while the attacker needs only ONE way in.	w4
What is Bruce Schneier's quote about good attacks?	"A good attack is one that the engineers never thought of."	w4
What are the three golden rules of computer security (Robert Morris)?	Do not own a computer; do not power it on; do not use it — which sacrifices the availability part of the triad.	w4
