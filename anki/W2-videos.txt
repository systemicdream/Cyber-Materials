Who gave the "No Tech Hacking" talk at 2600's The Last HOPE (2008)?	Johnny Long ("I'm Johnny. I hack stuff") — professional hacker, security researcher, and founder of Hackers For Charity	no-tech-hacking w2
What is "no-tech hacking"?	Compromising security without using technology or hacking tools — relying on observation, human carelessness, and the physical world	no-tech-hacking w2
What is dumpster diving?	Searching a target's trash for sensitive information (documents, passwords, discarded hardware) — a classic no-tech hacking technique	no-tech-hacking w2
What is shoulder surfing?	Directly observing someone as they enter a password, PIN, or sensitive data — e.g., watching keystrokes or on-screen information	no-tech-hacking w2
What is tailgating (piggybacking)?	Following an authorized person through a secure door or checkpoint without authenticating yourself — a physical-security attack	no-tech-hacking w2
What is social engineering?	Manipulating people (rather than systems) into revealing information or performing actions that compromise security	no-tech-hacking w2
Why can no-tech attacks be as damaging as technical hacks?	Because the weakest link in security is often people and physical security, not technology — no firewall stops someone reading a password on a sticky note	no-tech-hacking w2
Who gave the TED talk "Fighting viruses, defending the net"?	Mikko Hypponen — computer security researcher who has spent his career fighting malware	malware w2
What was Brain, and when was it written?	The first PC virus (1986) — infected 5¼-inch floppy boot sectors; its code even contains the authors' names and contact info ("welcome to the dungeon — 1986 Basit & Amjad", a Pakistani address)	malware w2
Who wrote the Brain virus?	Basit and Amjad, two brothers in Pakistan — Hypponen tracked them down 25 years later and found them still there	malware w2
How did early 1980s–90s PC viruses differ from today's malware?	Early ones were written by hobbyists/teenagers and announced themselves (e.g., a centipede or walking figure across your screen); today's malware is written by organized criminal gangs for profit and hides itself	malware w2
Who writes most of today's malware, and why?	Organized criminal gangs — because it makes money (stolen banking credentials, credit card data, etc.)	malware w2
How do criminals monetize infected computers?	Banking trojans (steal from online bank accounts), keyloggers (record everything you type, especially online purchases and card details), and even selling infected PCs on underground markets	malware w2
What is a keylogger?	Malware that silently sits hidden on a computer and records everything typed — searches, emails, passwords, credit card numbers — and sends it to criminals	malware w2
Why do criminals most prize intercepting online-purchase sessions?	Because shoppers type their name, delivery address, credit card number, and security code — everything needed to buy goods with the stolen card	malware w2
How do cybercriminals exploit the internet's global nature?	They move operations from country to country (one jurisdiction to another) whenever a site is shut down, because there's no ability to police such operations globally	malware w2
What does the underground cybercrime ecosystem afford that old virus writers lacked?	Hired programmers and testers, SQL-database backends, and surveillance of security researchers — real money lets them invest in better attacks	malware w2
How did researchers trace one Russian malware author?	A dormant signature "k78rus" in the code — 78 is the city code of St. Petersburg; tracking contacts there led to a young criminal blogging about his Mercedes-Benz (plate k78rus)	malware w2
According to Hypponen, what matters more than antivirus and firewalls for stopping cybercrime?	Global international law enforcement — finding the criminal gangs and, even more importantly, reaching skilled people before they turn to crime and giving them opportunities to use their skills for good	malware w2
What warning does Hypponen give about our reliance on PLCs and computers?	Everything around us (factories, power plants, elevators) is run by computers/PLCs, so we need preparedness — the ability to keep working even when computers fail (continuity, backups)	malware w2
What is Stuxnet?	The first computer worm to target an industrial control system (e.g., a gas pipeline or power plant)	stuxnet w2
How did Stuxnet likely first enter the target environment?	An infected USB key was physically brought in — perhaps by an unknowing third-party contractor or an insider	stuxnet w2
How did Stuxnet spread between computers inside the target network?	Via shared USB keys between coworkers, network shares, and two software exploits	stuxnet w2
What is a PLC?	Programmable Logic Controller — a small computer that controls industrial machinery such as pumps and centrifuges; it has no display or keyboard and is programmed from a separate computer	stuxnet w2
What was Stuxnet's goal once inside a network?	To find the computer used to program a PLC, so it could reprogram the PLC and sabotage the industrial process it controls	stuxnet w2
What did each newly infected computer do for the attacker?	It sent a message back over the Internet with general system information, letting the attacker steer the worm until it found a PLC-programming computer	stuxnet w2
Why did Stuxnet steal design documents?	To learn how the industrial control system was built, so the attacker could send code that sabotages it (e.g., a pipeline exploding or a centrifuge spinning out of control)	stuxnet w2
What software do engineers use to program the Siemens PLCs that Stuxnet targeted?	Step 7 software (used to write code blocks like OB1 and download them to the PLC)	stuxnet w2
What did the Symantec PLC lab demonstration show?	After infecting the PC, re-downloading the identical program showed the correct 3-second pump timer on screen, but Stuxnet intercepted and rewrote the download — the pump ran indefinitely and the balloon exploded	stuxnet w2
What is the key lesson of the Stuxnet PLC demonstration?	The code an engineer sends to a PLC can be intercepted and rewritten on an infected computer, so the PLC behaves differently from what was programmed — while everything looks normal to the operator	stuxnet w2
Who gave the TED talk "Cracking Stuxnet, a 21st-century cyber weapon"?	Ralph Langner — industrial-control security expert who led the analysis of Stuxnet's payload	stuxnet w2
What was Stuxnet's target facility?	The Natanz uranium enrichment plant in Iran — and, per Langner's analysis, only Natanz	stuxnet w2
What is the basic plot of Stuxnet?	Infect a Windows notebook used by a maintenance engineer to configure the "gray box" (real-time control system); the dropper passes the payload to the PLC, which damages the centrifuges	stuxnet w2
How did Langner's team realize Stuxnet was a directed attack?	It behaved "like a lab rat that didn't like our cheese" — the dropper probed the PLC and did nothing unless a very specific configuration (and target program) was present	stuxnet w2
What structure did Stuxnet's payload code have?	Two "digital bombs" (a smaller and a bigger one) — about 15,000 lines of assembly-like code, professionally engineered with insider knowledge	stuxnet w2
What was the small digital warhead's job?	Rotor control: on one cascade, spin centrifuge rotors up and down to crack the rotors — slowly and creepily so maintenance engineers couldn't diagnose it	stuxnet w2
What was the big digital warhead's job?	Manipulating valves across six cascades	stuxnet w2
What number in the attack code matched the target, and what did it mean?	164 — the number of centrifuges in each cascade at Natanz (which are subdivided into 15 stages, also mirrored in the code)	stuxnet w2
How did Stuxnet hide the sabotage from operators and safety systems?	It intercepted sensor inputs (pressure, vibration) and fed the running control code fake, prerecorded "legitimate" data — like feeding a security camera prerecorded video during a heist	stuxnet w2
Why is compromising a digital safety system especially dangerous?	Safety systems react in milliseconds (e.g., opening relief valves on an overspeed turbine) where no human could — if they're fooled, the plant can blow up with nobody noticing	stuxnet w2
Why did Langner call Stuxnet's approach a template for a "cyber weapon of mass destruction"?	The attack code is generic — not specific to centrifuges — so it would work in power plants or factories, and the payload could be spread by conventional worm technology instead of USB	stuxnet w2
According to Langner, who was the leading force behind Stuxnet?	The United States ("the cyber superpower"), with Mossad involved — he said "fortunately," since otherwise the problems would be bigger	stuxnet w2
